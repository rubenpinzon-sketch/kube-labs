## Section 4 — What I Do Best
**Timestamp: 3:00 – 4:30**

[Lean forward slightly. This is where you show conviction.]

Let me tell you about three moments that I think define what I actually do best.

[pause]

The first one happened early in my time at Globant. A client's deployment pipeline was taking over forty minutes per release, and the development team had basically stopped doing continuous deployment — it was too painful. I sat down with the pipeline configuration, rebuilt the CI/CD flow using Azure DevOps, introduced parallel stages, added caching layers, and integrated SonarCloud for quality gates so feedback arrived early, not at the end. Within two sprints, we were deploying in under eight minutes. The team started shipping daily. That change wasn't just technical — it changed the culture of that delivery team.

[pause]

The second moment was a Kubernetes migration. A legacy monolith running on virtual machines, owned by a client who was nervous about containers, nervous about Kubernetes, nervous about all of it. I didn't just hand them a migration plan — I walked alongside them. I set up Grafana dashboards so they could see exactly what the cluster was doing at every moment. I ran workshops. I wrote runbooks they could actually use at 2 a.m. without calling anyone. By go-live, they were confident. That's the outcome I care about: not just a working system, but a team that owns it.

[slow down here]

And the third — this one is smaller in scale but maybe the most telling. A junior engineer on my team was stuck trying to understand why a Terraform plan kept failing in a staging environment. I could have just fixed it for him. Instead, I sat with him for an hour, walked through the state file, the provider configuration, the variable scope. He found the issue himself. [slight smile] He's a much stronger engineer today, and I think that's one of the best uses of a senior engineer's time.
